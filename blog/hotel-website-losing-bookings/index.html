5 Signs Your Hotel Website Is Losing You Bookings

Good reviews bring people to your door. A weak website is what makes them close the tab before they ever get inside.

You've got the reviews. Guests love the place, the location, the breakfast. But somewhere between someone finding you on Google and them actually booking, you're losing people — and it's rarely because of the room. It's the website standing between them and you.

Here are five things worth checking today.

1. It doesn't work properly on a phone

Most people looking for a place to stay are doing it from their phone, often while already traveling. If your site requires pinching and zooming to read a menu or see room rates, they'll leave before they even see what you offer.

2. There's no clear way to book

A phone number buried in a paragraph isn't a booking button. If it takes more than one glance to find "how do I actually reserve a room," most visitors won't bother looking further.

3. It's entirely dependent on a booking platform

If the only way to book is through Booking.com or MakeMyTrip, you're paying a 15–25% commission on every single guest who could have booked directly — for no reason other than they couldn't find another way in.

A website isn't a brochure. It's the difference between a guest booking with you, or booking with the platform that's taking a cut of your revenue.

4. The photos don't match the reviews

People read "beautiful views" in your reviews, then land on a page with three blurry, dark photos. The gap between what guests say and what they see makes them doubt everything else on the page too.

5. There's no way to reach you directly

No WhatsApp link, no simple contact form, no clear phone number. Every extra step between "interested" and "in touch with you" is a chance for someone to give up and book somewhere else instead.

None of this requires a huge rebuild. A clean, mobile-friendly site with a clear way to book direct usually pays for itself within the first few guests it saves you from OTA commission.
